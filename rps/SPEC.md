# PACA-RPS v1: three-action generalization of the framing effect (prespecified design and analysis)

**Status:** approved by Chris on 14 Nov 2026 and frozen as git tag `rps-v1` before any real session was collected.
**Written:** 13–14 Nov 2026, in response to the ICLR 2027 reviews of submission 47839 (request for a richer action space).
**Nature of the study:** a new, exploratory cross-game extension. Its prompts, opponent, schedule and analysis are fixed before collection. MP and temp2x2 data serve only as context and are never pooled with RPS rows.

## Question

In matching pennies, telling models about the adversary and prescribing independent 50/50 play made them *more* exploitable by Proteus. Does the same informed framing worsen realized performance in standard rock–paper–scissors against a three-action Proteus?

## Decisions recorded (Chris, 13 Nov 2026)

1. **Temperature.** Request 0 and 1 for all seven models, exactly as in the temp2x2 replication, even though Opus 4.8, GPT-5.4 and GPT-5.4-mini ignore the parameter (13 Nov manipulation check: `require_parameters` returns 404 for those three, and their temperature-0, temperature-1 and omitted arms are indistinguishable). For those three models, the "temperature" factor compares two samples of the provider default. The analysis reports this and adds a descriptive estimate on the four models that apply temperature. `require_parameters` is not sent, so routing matches temp2x2.
2. **Game.** Standard RPS with the two prompts below, as written (not a three-action matching game).
3. **Secondary outcome added:** expected payoff under the opponent's committed mixture.

## Design

**112 sessions: 7 models × 2 prompts × 2 requested temperatures × 4 sessions per cell × 200 rounds = 22,400 rounds.**

- **Models:** the same seven OpenRouter slugs as the MP study and temp2x2. Providers are unpinned and logged per trial. A slug match does not show the weights are the same as in July.
- **One fresh request per round:** system prompt plus user message, never a growing conversation. top_p is not sent and no reasoning parameter is sent. The token budget starts at 20,000; any `finish_reason = length` doubles it, up to 80,000.
- **Per model:** 8 adjacent minimalist/informed pairs, 4 at each temperature.
  - Temperature sequence A is `0 1 1 0 0 1 1 0`; B is its mirror.
  - Prompt order is `I M M I M I I M` (A) or its mirror (B).
  - Every combination gives two informed-first and two minimalist-first pairs at each temperature.
- **Assignment:** both temperature starts and both prompt orders occur within every developer, and temperature order is crossed with prompt order.

  | Model | Temp seq | Prompt seq |
  |---|---|---|
  | Claude Opus 4.8 | A (0 first) | A |
  | Claude Sonnet 4.6 | B (1 first) | B |
  | Claude Haiku 4.5 | B | A |
  | GPT-5.4 | A | B |
  | GPT-5.4-mini | B | A |
  | Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite | B | B |
  | Gemini 3 Flash Preview | A | A |

- **Seeds:** both sessions of a pair share one opponent RNG seed, derived as `Random("paca-rps-v1|20261113|<pair>")`. There are 56 distinct seeds, none of them in the MP or temp2x2 sequences.
- **Schedule:** generated by `make_config.py` with no randomness (byte-identical on reruns), stored in `run_config.json` and `schedule.csv`.

  - Schedule SHA-256: `ecdbcab2dc4963f2…`
  - Full hashes are in the files and in `preflight_result.json`.

## Frozen prompts (`rps/rps_prompts.py`, version `paca-rps-v1`)

The texts are verbatim as supplied on 13 Nov 2026. They are asserted by tests, by the preflight and by every session.

- **Minimalist,** SHA-256 `504f18b15106218050ccce83c1858bd8dbbee54398b6a2f2ebeae6fbe4c051ef`: *"Choose the object that defeats the one on which the blue circle lands. / Rock defeats scissors, scissors defeats paper, and paper defeats rock. / Available actions: ROCK PAPER SCISSORS / You may briefly think through your choice. End your response with the chosen action on its own line."*
- **Informed,** SHA-256 `d0fe9e83464cc7bf62b766bb55fe2fa8a686223611a06b011984db41433461b6`: the RPS analogue of the MP informed prompt. It names the exploitative opponent and prescribes 1/3 each, independent across trials, with the warning about alternation, repetition and cycling. It contains no die analogy.
- **User message:** identical in layout to the MP builder (`prompts.build_prompt`). The last 5 rounds are described in words ("You chose ROCK - blue circle landed on PAPER"), followed by the last 25 as a compact R/P/S grid, oldest to most recent. The 5 described rounds are the last 5 of the grid.
- **Parser:** mirrors MP. The last occurrence of ROCK, PAPER or SCISSORS (case-insensitive) wins; otherwise a single R/P/S letter as the final line counts. Anything else is a parse failure, never a guess. An audit field records whether the final line agrees with the parsed choice.

**Known limitation (acknowledged when choosing RPS).** The minimalist prompt states the beats-cycle, which models will recognize as rock–paper–scissors. The minimal condition therefore reveals the game, though not the opponent or the equilibrium, so the contrast is weaker than in MP.

## Payoffs

The subject scores +1 for a win (R beats S, P beats R, S beats P), −1 for a loss and 0 for a tie; the opponent gets the negative. The null benchmark is uniform i.i.d. play: expected payoff 0, with win, loss and tie each at 1/3 against any opponent that cannot see the current choice. The study measures exploitation by *this* opponent, not formal exploitability.

## RPS Proteus v1 (`rps/rps_proteus.py`)

This is a prespecified adaptation of the binary opponent. It is not Lee et al.'s Algorithm 2 and is not claimed to be identical.

- **Nine channels,** all computed from rounds 1..t−1 only:
  - channel 0: the subject's marginal counts;
  - channels 1–4: the subject's next action following the current last k = 1..4 actions;
  - channels 5–8: the subject's next action following the current last k (action, W/L/D outcome) pairs.

  Only occurrences that have a completed follower are counted.
- **Detection:** for each channel, three exact two-sided binomial tests (count of R, P and S vs 1/3) are combined as p_adj = min(1, 3·min p). The channel is active if p_adj < .05. No minimum n is imposed; the smallest follower sample that can activate is n = 4, all the same action.
- **Response:**
  - Each active channel's gain is g = max over the opponent's actions of EV, where EV_R = p_S − p_P, EV_P = p_R − p_S and EV_S = p_P − p_R.
  - The active channel with the largest g wins; ties go to the lowest index.
  - The opponent plays g·one_hot(best response) + (1−g)·uniform, using one RNG draw per trial. Ties in the best response go to R, then P, then S.
  - With no active channel, play is uniform, logged as `random_no_detection`.
- **Relation to MP:** with two actions this rule is exactly the binary opponent's. There g = 2p − 1, the channel with the largest bias wins, and the opponent plays the opposite side with probability p.
- **Difference from MP:** the three tests and ×3 correction per channel make each RPS channel somewhat more conservative than the single binary test.

## Calibration (offline, `rps/calibrate_rps.py`, results in `rps/calibration/`)

**Null player:** 1,000 sessions of uniform i.i.d. play against the live opponent code.
- Payoff −0.0020 (SE 0.0019; 95% CI [−0.0056, +0.0017]); W/L/D .332/.334/.334.
- Any-channel activation 0.099 of trials. The MP binary opponent on the same metric, 1,000 fair-coin sessions, gives 0.107.
- Activation by channel: ch0 .035, ch1 .031, ch2 .021, ch3 .009, ch4 .002, ch5 .020, ch6 .002, ch7 and ch8 ≈ 0. The high-order outcome contexts are sparse.
- False-activation episodes have a median length of 1 trial and a mean of 1.8.

A payoff of 0 against the null player holds for any opponent that cannot see the current choice, so this checks the code rather than the detector. Activation is the informative null quantity.

**Structured players** (200 sessions each; payoff per round, with the median trial of first activation and the main channel):

| Player | Payoff | First activation (trial) | Main channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Always rock | −0.98 | 5 | ch0 |
| R/P alternation | −0.95 | 10 | ch1 |
| R→P→S cycle | −0.93 | 14 | ch1 |
| Noisy cycle (.75) | −0.56 | 16 | ch1, ch5 |
| Biased .6/.2/.2 | −0.19 | 14 | ch0, ch1 |
| Win-stay / lose-shift | −0.85 | 22 | ch5, ch4 |
| Beat the opponent's last move | −0.84 | 22 | ch5, ch4 |
| Never repeat (repeat rate 0; the MP over-alternation analogue) | −0.22 | 22 | ch1, ch2 |
| Repeat rate .20 (mild over-alternation) | **−0.02** | 44 | ch1 |

The detector exploits every major regularity. It is weak against mild over-alternation: a repeat rate of .20 instead of .33 costs only about 0.02 per round.

**Expected payoff:** its session-level variance is 3% of realized payoff's under the null player, and 16–67% for the structured players. It is therefore the more precise secondary measure of the same quantity.

## Integrity rules

- **No substituted choices.** A parse failure, an API failure after 3 network attempts, or a truncation unresolved at the 80,000-token ceiling aborts the session. The partial file and an error record go to `data_rps_v1/_errors/`, and the whole session is rerun from round 1 with the same index and seed.
- **Attempt cap:** at most 3 attempts per session. If a session still fails, that model's stream stops (later sessions are not run out of order), and the decision goes to Chris.
- **Atomic writes:** a session is written as `.partial` and renamed only after all 200 rows. Raw API responses go to `.raw.jsonl`.
- **Replay check:** every complete file is replayed by `verify_session.py`, which reconstructs every user message, opponent decision, channel state and payoff from the seed and the logged choices.
- **Pilot data** (`data_rps_v1_pilot/`, session index +9000, seed +500000) are never analyzed with the real run.

## Prespecified analysis (`rps/analyze_rps.py`)

The unit of inference is the model (n = 7). For each model and temperature T, D_T is the mean of its four paired differences (informed − minimalist) in mean payoff per round.

**Primary:** D_1 for payoff (requested temperature 1), reported as:
- the mean over the seven models;
- **the t(6) 95% CI, the inferential criterion;**
- a two-sided t-test;
- an exact Wilcoxon signed-rank test;
- the number of models with a negative effect.

| Result | Reading |
|---|---|
| Negative, CI excludes 0 | supported |
| Negative, CI includes 0 | suggestive only, not confirmed |
| Positive | reported as such |

**Secondary:**
1. D_0 for payoff.
2. The interaction D_1 − D_0, with its t(6) CI. No equality claims are made from nonsignificance.
3. Expected payoff: D_1, D_0 and the interaction.
4. Win, loss and tie rates.
5. Marginal bias: TV = ½Σ|p_a − 1/3| and max |p_a − 1/3|, with all three frequencies.
6. Sequential dependence: the 3×3 transition matrix, the repeat rate, the forward-cycle rate (the next action defeats the previous one) and the backward-cycle rate. The prespecified scalar is **excess lag-1 mutual information**: plug-in MI between consecutive actions minus its mean under uniform i.i.d. play of length 200, estimated from 20,000 Monte Carlo sequences with seed 20261113 (≈ 0.0147 bits).
7. The detector: activation by channel, selected channels and sample sizes.
8. Descriptive checks:
   - the order check (informed-first vs minimalist-first pairs) at each T;
   - position in the stream;
   - providers;
   - leave-one-model-out and developer-out estimates of the primary;
   - the primary, D_0 and the interaction restricted to the four models that apply temperature (n = 4).

**Hard acceptance checks**, printed by the analysis:
- 7 models, 2 prompts and 2 temperatures, with 4 complete sessions per cell;
- 112 sessions and 22,400 trials;
- 0 non-model choices;
- all replays pass;
- one system-prompt hash per prompt.

**Dry run.** All 112 scheduled sessions were run through the real session runner with a fake model: uniform under the minimalist prompt, never-repeat under the informed one. The analysis recovered the planted effect: D_1 = −0.235, compared with the calibration's −0.22 for a never-repeat player. Expected payoff gave a CI of ±0.013, against ±0.042 for realized payoff, and all acceptance checks passed. Command: `rps/tests/fake_dry_run.py`.

**Written after collection:** the figures (net payoff by prompt, payoff vs TV, payoff vs excess MI) and the appendix text. They do not change any test above.
